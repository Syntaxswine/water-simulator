The colour scale is fixed, never auto-ranged: auto-ranging looks better and lies, because a calm frame gets stretched until numerical noise fills the range. The colour ramp is symmetric in CIE lightness about mean sea level, so a crest and a trough of equal size are equally visible — measured, not asserted; see the header of src/render.mjs for what the previous ramp actually did.

Cells per wavelength is reported offshore → shallow end, and the warning is on the SHALLOW one. L scales with sqrt(h), so a shoaling wave is resolved worse the closer it gets to the beach: the offshore figure is the flattering one and it is not what decides whether a wave height means anything. The shallow end is taken at the shallowest wet cell, but no shallower than the depth-limited breaking depth H/0.78 — past that the wave is a bore and a wavelength criterion no longer describes it.

The globe is drawn by INVERSE ORTHOGRAPHIC ray-sphere sampling: for every screen pixel inside the disc, z = √(1 − x² − y²), rotate to (lon, lat), sample the cell that contains it. That is not an approximation of the projection, it is the projection. The pixel → (lon, lat) table is baked once per size — rotation only ADDS a constant to longitude and cannot change a latitude — and node src/globe.mjs checks the table against the live computation to the bit, over the whole disc at eight rotations, with a one-ULP control to show the check can fail. 108 checks, including the two that matter: a resting ocean must be a single flat colour at all 24 rotations, which any shading term breaks; and over a field with a wave in it, every painted byte must be surfaceColour(b + h − msl, scale), which is what pins the sign, the scale and the rotation. Both are needed — a renderer that always paints mean sea level scores a perfect zero on the resting-ocean test alone, and for a while one that inverted the sign did too. GlobeView.draw() itself is run against a recording canvas, so the graticule, the limb stroke and the terminator are checked at the coordinates they are drawn at.
